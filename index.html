Healthcare Data Analytics Extern

58 CA counties · Python, pandas, Chart.js · Difference-in-Differences

Did California's 2022 Medi-Cal expansion actually improve diabetes outcomes, or just improve detection?

I gathered county-level Medi-Cal enrollment, claims, and diagnosis data across all 58 California counties from multiple state and public health sources, standardizing formats and cross-referencing entries to catch discrepancies before running any analysis.

I applied a Difference-in-Differences model in Python to isolate the policy's effect on diagnosis rates, tested the parallel-trends assumption the design depends on, and checked my results against existing public health literature before trusting them.

What I found The rural–urban diabetes diagnosis gap narrowed after expansion, but the enrollment–outcome relationship itself wasn't statistically significant (p = 0.588). The likelier story: expansion increased detection, not underlying health outcomes, an important distinction for anyone deciding where to target follow-up investment.

Amazon
People Analytics Extern

~250 reviews + 30 transcripts · Python, NLTK, TextBlob

Where is burnout actually costing Amazon fulfillment centers the most?

I collected and cleaned unstructured employee feedback from roughly 250 Glassdoor reviews and 30 YouTube video transcripts, standardizing the text with Python, NLTK, and TextBlob so it could be reliably coded into consistent themes and compared across sources.

I ran sentiment analysis and a root-cause/priority scoring framework across three worker segments (full-time, part-time, and specialized roles) to find where an intervention would have the most leverage.

What I found Full-time warehouse associates scored highest on every risk dimension, driven mainly by safety and pay & benefits mentions. I translated that into a two-week pilot proposal, a floor-level "pacing credit" system, with a defined success metric and a binary go/no-go decision point, presented to a leadership audience.
